Featured Project — Senior Seminar Capstone

“A comparison of pH, nitrate and phosphate levels found in eutrophied and non-eutrophied ocean zones.”

Summary

Compared pH, nitrate, and phosphate levels between eutrophied and non-eutrophied North American coastal sites using NOAA CODAP-NA data. Findings indicated higher nitrate and phosphate in eutrophied zones and regionally lower pH, suggesting eutrophication may intensify local acidification. Methods included site-pair selection, depth control from 0–200 m, data reduction, and ANOVA in Excel.

Senior Seminar Capstone

NOAA Water Quality Analysis: Eutrophication & Ocean Acidification

A compact research-poster view of my capstone comparing pH, nitrate, and phosphate levels in eutrophied and non-eutrophied North American coastal waters.

Research Question

Do pH, nitrate, and phosphate levels differ between eutrophied and non-eutrophied North American ocean zones?

Hypothesis

Eutrophied zones were expected to show lower pH and higher nitrate and phosphate levels compared with non-eutrophied zones.

28,208 Original NOAA samples
780 Focused research samples
7 Eutrophied/non-eutrophied site pairs
0–200 m Depth range analyzed

Data source: NOAA CODAP-NA. Site selection compared eutrophication hotspots, coastline location, and ocean circulation context.

Conclusion

Nitrate and phosphate concentrations were higher in eutrophied zones, supporting the idea that nutrient enrichment is tied to eutrophication. pH patterns suggested lower pH in eutrophied waters overall, with the clearest pattern in West Coast sites. The project also highlighted limits in comparing East and West Coast samples collected during different years.

Methods Snapshot

The project used NOAA CODAP-NA secondary data and selected variables including longitude, latitude, date, cruise ID, depth, nitrate, phosphate, and calculated in-situ pH. Data were narrowed to samples from 0–200 meters, where light supports photosynthesis. Two-way ANOVAs without replication were used to compare eutrophied versus non-eutrophied sites and East versus West Coast groupings.
